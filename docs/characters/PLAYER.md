# The player (Maple / Cedar / Sumac)

Character sheet for the player character, workshopped with the owner on
2026-10-20. Male or female, same voice.

## In one line
A wisecracking professor who truly cares about everyone in their life, and
uses humor to make hard or absurd moments lighter, as if they half-know
they're in a sitcom.

## Three words
Wry, warm, absurd.

## Relationships
| Who | How the player sees them |
|---|---|
| **Ivy** | Their wife and research partner. Loves making her laugh; a loving married double act. |
| **Elm** | Not related; same age, but in the vein of a slightly younger brother: looks out for him, rolls with his craziness, teases him often but never harshly. |
| **Jenny** | Like a sister-in-law and a buddy. Doesn't tease her; they just get each other. "Thanks for making my brother happy." A different dynamic from Jenny and Ivy's. |
| **Oak** | Mentor and grandfather figure; one of his two best students (with Elm). |
| **Delia** | Their warm host. |

## Comedy toolbox
Sitcom-level jokes, for the whole game.

- **Ironic mock-disbelief:** faced with something obviously ill-judged, deadpan insists it *must* be deliberate. "No, surely those cables across the doorway are intentional. Trip-based security."
- **Dry understatement,** British-sitcom style. "So the plan is to fight the grass. Personally."
- **Absurd escalation:** "Three? I'll notify the authorities."
- **Left-field jokes:** out of nowhere, perfectly calm. "The second time was a sacrifice to the sea."
- **Puns and references:** nods to the anime and the games, never forced.
- **Inner asides:** short thoughts in parentheses that wink at the absurdity, like a gentle fourth-wall lean ("(I believe him about the dishes.)"). Subtle, never a literal break.
- **Light self-deprecation,** occasionally. Comedy is tragedy plus time, but kept gentle and on-tone.
- **Sitcom timing:** setup, a beat ("…"), punchline. Short.
- **Sincere when it counts:** the joke steps aside for a plain, warm line ("Elm. You called me every week.").

Comedy touchstones: Home Improvement, The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air, Frasier,
Friends, How I Met Your Mother, Keeping Up Appearances, George of the Jungle,
Looney Tunes, Deadpool's asides, the early Pokémon anime.

## They would never
- Be cruel, punch down, or joke at someone who's struggling. Humor is for lifting people up and pointing at the absurdity, not at the person.
- Give long speeches. One or two boxes.
- Go darker than the game's tone.
- Overtry: if a joke needs explaining, cut it.
- Take big decisions away from the person playing.
